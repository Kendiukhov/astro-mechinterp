Multimodal scientific foundation models are increasingly trained on raw observations alongside catalogue-derived products computed from them. We show, for AION-1—a 40-table transformer foundation model for astronomy trained on more than 2×10⁸ objects and released with open weights (Parker et al., 2025)—that this design choice creates a systematic failure: the model defers to the catalogue channel over the pixels, and does not discount it when the two conflict. Holding image tokens byte-identical and editing only the survey segmentation map moves every quantity the model reports—flux, size, ellipticity, redshift—by 110–4400 times a matched placebo; corrupting catalogue photometry instead leaves the model nine times worse than supplying no metadata at all. Causal interventions identify the mechanism as detection gating, presence at the field centre (r = 0.47) rather than the light the mask encloses (r = 0.30), and across 322 real blended systems the model ignores entirely how the pipeline partitioned the light (R = −0.006). We price the consequence: propagating the survey’s own measured 3.68% detection-miss rate, represented by the segmentation fields the pipeline actually returns when it misses, shifts tomographic mean redshifts by up to 0.68 times the LSST DESC requirement—one failure mode consuming two-thirds of the error budget—while observed positional errors take it to 8.3 times. Withholding the detection channel removes the vulnerability at no measurable cost, and the effect grows with model scale. Two further limits are in the tokeniser, not the transformer: the image codec resolves 28 effective states on source patches against 934 for the spectrum codec, and the redshift readout is quantisation-limited. Finally, sparse dictionaries are unreliable as causal handles on this target: across 15 dictionaries spanning width, sparsity and seed, recovery of the gated readout spans 26–75% and moves by up to 18 points on the seed alone, thirteen of fifteen lose to difference-in-means, and reconstruction quality does not predict which dictionary will steer.
