To the Editors, Astronomy and Computing

Dear Editors,

I submit "A survey detection channel overrides the pixels in an astronomical foundation model,
and biases tomographic mean redshifts" for consideration as a Full Length Article.

The paper measures a systematic that a survey pipeline introduces into a foundation model
trained on its outputs. AION-1 ingests the Legacy Survey segmentation map as an input modality.
Holding the image tokens byte-identical and editing only that map changes every quantity the
model reports - flux, size, ellipticity and redshift - by 110-4400 times a matched placebo, and
causal interventions identify the mechanism as detection gating rather than masked aperture
photometry. The consequence is priced against a survey requirement: the pipeline's own measured
3.68% detection-miss rate, propagated with misses represented by the segmentation fields the
pipeline actually returns when it misses, shifts tomographic mean redshifts by a median 0.71
times the LSST DESC requirement over 40 miss assignments and exceeds it in 12 of them. Observed
positional errors take the worst bin to 8.3 times. The paper ends with an operational
recommendation that costs nothing measurable: withhold the detection channel when the detection
cannot be vouched for.

I believe this sits within the scope the journal describes when it encourages "reports on
practice which describe the outcomes (positive and negative) of the practical application of
informatics techniques within astronomy research and operations", and under the listed topics of
astrostatistics and machine learning, data processing pipelines and automated systems, and
astroinformatics and analytics. The work is an application of computing to astronomy with a
consequence for survey operations, rather than computer science research: the interpretability
machinery is the instrument, and the measured quantity is a redshift systematic.

Two precedents in the journal are the closest antecedents I am aware of, and both are cited in
the manuscript: Sensitivity probes of deep networks used for lens finding in DES imaging
(Astronomy and Computing 2021, doi:10.1016/j.ascom.2021.100535), and systematic biases arising
when deep networks annotate large catalogues of astronomical images (Astronomy and Computing
2022, doi:10.1016/j.ascom.2022.100545).

Three disclosures.

First, a preprint of this manuscript is posted as arXiv:2608.23626, under a CC BY 4.0 licence. I
note the licence explicitly so that it is on the record at submission. I select the subscription
publishing route and am not requesting open access.

Second, the study audits a model released by the Polymathic AI collaboration. I have no
affiliation with, funding from, or other connection to that group, and no competing interest to
declare. I would ask that referees be chosen from outside that collaboration. The manuscript is
framed throughout as a property of catalogue-conditioned training data that any similarly
trained model would inherit, not as a criticism of one group's work, and the study was only
possible because they released weights, tokenisers and code openly.

Third, a large language model was used substantially in this work, to write the analysis code,
run the experiments, produce the figures and draft the text. This is declared in the manuscript
in the form the journal prescribes, and because the use extended into the research process it is
also described in the methods section, together with the steps taken to validate it: every
numeric claim is checked programmatically against the recorded output that produced it, every
figure is regenerated from that output with no model call, and the full checking apparatus is in
the public repository.

All code, per-experiment outputs and the manuscript source are available at
https://github.com/Kendiukhov/astro-mechinterp, with a table mapping every numbered claim in the
paper to the script and output file that produced it. The data are public and the repository
documents how to rebuild them.

The manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere.

Yours sincerely,
